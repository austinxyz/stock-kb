## 1. 赛道定义 + 价值链

Low-Earth-orbit (LEO) satellite is the buildout of large constellations in the ~300–2,000 km orbital band to deliver broadband, direct-to-cell connectivity, Earth observation, and defense/ISR services with far lower latency than legacy geostationary (GEO) satellites. The investment theme rides the "Starlink economy" — falling launch costs (reusable rockets) compounding with dual commercial + defense demand. Names span pre-revenue narrative bets (ASTS) to launch infrastructure (RKLB) to imagery/data (PL).

| Layer | What it does | Key players |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| Launch & access to orbit | Rockets, rideshare, launch cadence | RKLB, SIDU |
| Satellite manufacturing / buses | Builds the spacecraft & constellations | RKLB (Space Systems), ASTS, SIDU |
| Direct-to-cell / connectivity | Broadband + cellular-to-phone service | ASTS, GSAT, SATS |
| Earth observation / data | Imagery, geospatial analytics | PL, SIDU |
| Components / niche suppliers | Sub-systems, propulsion, optics | YSS, GCTS |
| Investable ETFs (space economy) | Whole-chain space exposure | UFO, ARKX, ROKT |

## 2. 核心赛道问题

> Can falling launch costs + the direct-to-cell connectivity wave drive at least one investable pure-play (ASTS) to durable, scaled recurring carrier revenue ($300M+/yr) before SpaceX Starlink's vertically integrated, capital-advantaged constellation forecloses the commercial runway — and before pre-revenue narrative names mean-revert?

The thesis is structurally sound (launch-cost deflation is real and durable); the binding constraint is that the cleanest pure-plays are pre-revenue and richly valued, so this is an entry-timing and survivorship problem, not a thesis-validity problem. **Commercial viability timelines have extended vs. original expectations; SpaceX consolidation is accelerating, reducing the number of viable pure-play investment vehicles.**

- **Launch cost deflation**: reusable launch has driven $/kg-to-orbit down by ~1–2 orders of magnitude over the past decade — the structural cost curve that makes large LEO constellations economic. *[估算]*

> Growth lever is dual: launch-cost deflation (supply side) × greenfield direct-to-cell + defense demand (demand side).

## 5. 结构性驱动力

1. **Launch-cost deflation** — reusable rockets (and a competitive launch market incl. RKLB Neutron) keep driving $/kg to orbit down, making large constellations economically viable. This is the foundational secular driver.
2. **Direct-to-cell connectivity** — unmodified smartphones connecting straight to satellites (ASTS 3GPP architecture; Starlink Direct-to-Cell) opens a greenfield TAM with carrier wholesale economics.
3. **Defense / national-security demand** — proliferated LEO (SDA, ISR, resilient comms) is a multi-year government procurement tailwind that de-risks commercial-only demand for several names (RKLB, PL).
4. **Earth observation + AI analytics** — geospatial data (PL) gains value as AI makes imagery analytics cheaper and more actionable.

*Theme vs cycle:* drivers 1–3 are structural multi-year secular (launch economics + greenfield demand + government budgets); the near-term price action is cyclical/sentiment-driven and in active correction.

## 7. 价值链卡位

Profit pools differ sharply by layer. **Launch (RKLB)** is the durable toll-road — every constellation operator must buy access to orbit — but is capital-intensive and competing against SpaceX's reusable cost advantage. **Direct-to-cell connectivity (ASTS)** has the largest TAM and the strongest moat-if-it-works (3GPP multi-carrier architecture, Tier-1 carrier wholesale deals with AT&T/Verizon/Rakuten/Vodafone) but the weakest balance-sheet durability (FCF −$1.19B FY2025) and faces SpaceX head-on. **Earth observation (PL)** captures recurring data revenue but in a more commoditizing market.

**Competitive context:** SpaceX/Starlink is the 800-lb gorilla — vertically integrated (owns launch), capital-advantaged, and now pushing its own Direct-to-Cell. The sector is consolidating around SpaceX's dominance, which is reducing the viable pure-play investment universe. The investable question is whether ASTS can lock up enough Tier-1 carriers (switching-cost moat) before Starlink pivots fully carrier-agnostic. US names dominate the investable universe; this is not a China-competition story the way packaging or optical is.
