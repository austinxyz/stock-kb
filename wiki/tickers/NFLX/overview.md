## Moat

**Wide and widening.**

### What NFLX has
- **Content scale**: $20B+ FY26 content spend across 50+ country production hubs — ≈3× any pure-streaming peer
- **Recommendation algorithm**: industry-best engagement-per-member, reduces churn and content $/engaged-hour
- **Open Connect CDN**: lowest cost-to-stream globally, structural margin advantage
- **In-house ad-tech (Netflix Ads Suite)**: captures take-rate previously lost to intermediaries; 4,000+ advertisers, +70% YoY (as of Q1 26)
- **Live/sports flywheel**: NFL Christmas (year 3), WWE Raw, FIFA Women's World Cup 2027

### What NFLX doesn't have
- **Attention-share dominance**: YouTube holds ~10× the daily watch hours of all SVOD combined among teens — the live competitive threat, not subscriber share
- **Mature-market subscriber growth**: US/UK/NL saturating; growth must come from ARM + ad-tier mix, not new sign-ups
- **Immunity to sports-rights cost inflation**: NBA/NFL renewal cycles are a discretionary, uncertain-ROI investment

### Verdict
**Wide moat.** Content scale + algorithm + CDN + ad-tech in-housing form a durable, multi-year advantage over DIS+/Max/Prime. The real competitive question is attention-share (YouTube/TikTok), which affects ad-inventory growth, not subscriber retention — a real risk but not (yet) a thesis-break.

## Pivotal Investment Question

> **Does Netflix's advertising business scale to $7–10B by 2028 at near-software-margin economics — combined with low-double-digit ARM growth and continued mid-single-digit subscriber growth — to compound revenue 12–14% with operating leverage to ≈33% margin, justifying a sustained 30–35× P/E? Or does ad revenue plateau around $5B as YouTube/TikTok continue capturing attention share, content cost re-accelerates on sports rights, and engagement growth stalls — re-rating the multiple to 22–25× and the stock toward $70–80?**

Q2 FY2026 gave a genuinely mixed read: EPS beat, operating margin (33.4%) well above the FY guide, and **viewing hours accelerated** (+2% H1 2026 vs +1.5% H1 2025 — a data point that cuts against the Nielsen US-share-decline narrative). But Q3 guide ($12.86B, +11.7%) landed below Street's ~$13B, and analyst targets were cut sharply (median $115→$92) — the market's read leans toward the bear-adjacent scenario on growth *rate*, even as engagement itself looks fine.
