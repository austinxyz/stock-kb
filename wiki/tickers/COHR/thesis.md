## 1. Why Does This Company Exist? + Pivotal Investment Question

Coherent Corp. exists at the physical layer of the internet — it makes the glass, lasers, and electronic-photonic components that convert electrical signals to light and back, enabling data to move at scale inside data centers, between cities, and across continents. Founded in 1971 as II-VI Incorporated, the company spent five decades accumulating compound semiconductor expertise (InP, GaAs, SiC). The January 2022 acquisition of Coherent Inc. (formerly JDSU, then Viavi) created the world's #2 optical components company and positioned COHR directly in the path of the AI infrastructure build-out.

The structural shift: AI model training and inference requires orders-of-magnitude more data movement inside data centers compared to conventional cloud workloads. The hyperscalers (AWS, Google, Meta, Microsoft) are upgrading from 400G to 800G optical interconnects now, with 1.6T on the near-term roadmap. COHR is a qualified supplier to all four. Each AI GPU cluster generates demand for thousands of optical transceivers that COHR manufactures.

**Pivotal Investment Question**: *Can Coherent Corp. sustain 20%+ annual revenue growth through FY2028 — converting AI data center optical demand into margin expansion to 40%+ gross margins — while deleveraging $2.5B+ in debt, before Chinese photonic IC makers erode its 800G/1.6T competitive position?*

Current milestone status: Revenue acceleration confirmed (Q3 FY26: $1,806M GAAP, +20.4% YoY). Margin improvement confirmed — GAAP GM% 35.2% → 36.6% → 36.9% → **37.6%** over 4 quarters (non-GAAP reported ~38.5%); expansion trajectory intact. Debt paydown on track ($600M reduced since merger). Binary: Does the margin trajectory reach 40%+ by FY2027, or does commoditization halt it at 38-39%? Q4 FY26 (8/12) is the next confirmation point.

## 5. Competitive Moat

**What makes COHR defensible:**
- Manufacturing scale: Building a compound semiconductor fab (InP, GaAs) takes 7-10 years and billions in capital. COHR has this; most competitors do not
- Hyperscaler qualifications: Each hyperscaler has a 12-18 month qualification process for new optical module suppliers. COHR is qualified at all four major hyperscalers — this is a multi-year competitive barrier for new entrants
- Vertical integration: From wafer growth → component fabrication → module assembly → testing. Enables superior quality control versus purely fabless optical companies

**What is eroding the moat:**
- Chinese optical module makers (Innolight, Accelink, HiSilicon) have qualified at some hyperscalers and compete on price for commodity 400G and lower-tier 800G
- Co-packaged optics (CPO) technology — where optical components are integrated directly into GPUs/ASICs — could eventually reduce the standalone transceiver TAM in 3-5 years
- Intel Photonics (silicon photonics) and Broadcom (optical) are investing in integrated photonic approaches that could reduce reliance on compound semiconductor modules

**Direct Competitors**

| Company | Revenue | Strength | Weakness |
|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| Lumentum (LITE) | $2.1B | Pure optical, 65%+ growth, higher margins | Smaller scale; more telecom exposure |
| Ciena (CIEN) | $5.1B | Full optical networking systems | Systems integrator, not component maker |
| Innolight (private) | ~$1B est | Chinese cost advantage; fast-follower | Not listed; geopolitical risk cuts both ways |
| Intel Photonics | N/A | Silicon photonics IP; co-packaged future | Non-core to Intel; execution risk |
| Neophotonics | Acquired by Intel 2023 | InP expertise | Absorbed into Intel |

**Verdict**: Narrow moat. The hyperscaler qualification barrier and manufacturing scale provide 2-3 year defensibility. Beyond FY2028, silicon photonics commoditization and Chinese competition are material risks to premium margins. Monitor Chinese market share at hyperscalers as leading indicator.

## 9. Industry-Specific Macro Analysis

**TAM**: Optical transceiver market for data centers estimated at $12B in 2024, growing to $25-30B by 2028 (CAGR ~20-22%). Global optical components total addressable market (including telecom) is ~$30B today, growing to $60B+ by 2030 (Source: Lightcounting, Dell'Oro estimates).

**End-market dynamics**:
- AI datacenters (800G/1.6T): Fastest-growing sub-market; 40-50% annual growth in optical transceiver volumes. Hyperscalers committed to $200B+ combined 2025 CapEx.
- Telecom (ROADM, coherent long-haul): Bottomed in FY2024; gradual recovery as carriers upgrade 5G backhaul and submarine cable.
- Industrial lasers: Stable; semiconductor capital equipment spending correlates with node transitions.
- SiC (Silicon Carbide): Slower than expected due to EV demand normalization; less important for near-term thesis.

**Competitive dynamics**:
- US-China export controls (2022-2024) reduced COHR's exposure to Chinese telecom customers but also restricted Chinese competitors' access to advanced US equipment.
- The 800G market is currently supply-constrained — hyperscalers are buying all available production.
- The 1.6T market begins ramping in 2026; first mover qualifications are critical.
