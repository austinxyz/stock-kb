## Moat

**Wide (network effects + subscription flywheel)**

### What UBER has
- **Two-sided network density**: sub-3-minute pickup in core markets creates an active liquidity barrier; new entrants face cold-start problem
- **Uber One flywheel**: 50M members (+50% YoY) spending 3× non-members; >50% of consolidated bookings from members — structural demand-side switching cost
- **Data/algorithm advantage**: 13.6B trips FY2025, ~$1T cumulative gross bookings — pricing, fraud, ETA models trained at scale no entrant can match
- **Capital asymmetry**: $9.8B FCF → can fund new-market entry, AV prepayments, and absorb regulatory penalties; buyback $27B authorization
- **AV distribution positioning + owned-stack stakes**: 30+ AV partners (Waymo, Zoox, Hertz/Nuro/Lucid, Aurora) PLUS, as of May 2026, **$10B+ owned-stack commitments** (Lucid/Nuro, Rivian, Wayve, Avride, Baidu) — Uber is buying control of more of the AV value chain, but trading the asset-light framing for capital-committed AV operator risk

### What UBER doesn't have
- **Delivery market leadership in US**: DoorDash retains ~65% restaurant share; Uber Eats is structural #2
- **Driver loyalty**: drivers multi-home across Uber + Lyft; supply-side lock-in is soft
- **Clear AV revenue line**: management hasn't disclosed AV take — the entire AV bull case is unquantified
- **Single CFO stability**: third CFO in three years (Krishnamurthy effective Feb 2026)

### Verdict
**Wide moat in two-sided network + US Mobility; narrow in Delivery; existential question in AV (could widen dramatically or erode if OEMs bypass)**. Moat has been durable 2017–present; AV resolution is the binary that determines 5-year value.

## Pivotal Investment Question

> **Does Uber's distribution moat (199M MAPCs, 50M Uber One members, real-time matching across 70+ countries) make it the indispensable layer for autonomous-vehicle commercialization — or does AV economics ultimately let OEMs/Waymo bypass Uber and capture the consumer relationship directly?**

Market priced "bypass" in late 2024 (–35% from highs). The 30+ AV partnership flurry (Waymo Phoenix/Austin, Zoox Vegas/LA, Hertz/Nuro SF) is the rebuttal. **May 2026 sharpened — and complicated — the question**: Uber broke from Waymo and committed $10B+ to *funding Waymo's competitors* (Lucid/Nuro, Rivian, Wayve, Avride, Baidu) while studying a full Delivery Hero takeover. Uber is now both a Waymo distributor and a Waymo competitor — buying supply-stack control, but adding a capital-intensity/ROIC question to the bear case.
