## Moat

**Narrow — scale and breadth, partially offset by commoditization.**

### What VSH has
- **Broadest component portfolio in class**: 6 product segments (MOSFETs, Diodes, Optoelectronics, Resistors, Inductors, Capacitors) — one-stop-shop for design engineers
- **Owned global fab footprint**: Manufacturing assets in US, Germany, Israel, Asia — hard to replicate at scale
- **62-year track record**: Established in 1962; 20+ brand names with deep OEM relationships (automotive, industrial, defense/aerospace, healthcare)
- **Low-voltage MOSFET IP**: Siliconix heritage gives leadership in low-power switching applications

### What VSH doesn't have
- **Pricing power**: Passive components and discrete semiconductors are commodity-adjacent; Chinese competitors (Yageo, Murata, Semtech China) provide relentless pricing pressure
- **Gross margin resilience**: GM% collapsed 9 points from 2022 cycle peak to 2025 trough — evidence of limited pricing protection
- **AI/data center premium**: VSH's products enter at the power-supply and signal level, not at the AI accelerator level; no narrative uplift from AI capex

### Verdict
**Narrow moat.** Scale, breadth, and brand longevity provide switching-cost stickiness with incumbent OEM customers. However, commodity nature of most products limits pricing power. Moat widens during supply crunches; erodes during demand troughs.
