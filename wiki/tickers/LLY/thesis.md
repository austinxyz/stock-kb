## 1. Why Does This Company Exist? + Pivotal Investment Question

**Founding insight**: Disease causes suffering. Eli Lilly was founded in 1876 to manufacture standardized medicines in an era when drug quality was unreliable. 150 years later, Lilly captured the biggest pharmaceutical insight of the decade: GLP-1 receptor agonists (incretins) could address not just type 2 diabetes, but the global obesity epidemic. The dual GIP/GLP-1 agonism of tirzepatide — delivering superior weight loss (22.5% vs. semaglutide's 15%) — made LLY the highest-market-cap pharmaceutical company in history at its peak.

**The tirzepatide revolution**: Tirzepatide (Mounjaro for T2D, Zepbound for obesity) is the fastest-scaling pharmaceutical franchise in history: ~$0.5B in FY2022 → ~$40B estimated FY2025. Global obesity affects 500–750M adults; current GLP-1 penetration is <5%. The penetration curve from 5% → 20% over a decade is the structural thesis.

**Current strategic vision**: Extend tirzepatide across all obesity-adjacent indications (OSA — approved, OA — positive data, cardiovascular, MASH); launch Foundayo (oral GLP-1, approved April 1, 2026) to capture needle-averse patients; advance Retatrutide (triple agonist, Phase 3, 26.6% weight loss) as the next-generation franchise; build a $50B+ U.S. manufacturing moat.

**Pivotal Investment Question:**
> Can Lilly sustain a premium multiple (~32× FY2026E EPS) across the tirzepatide ramp and Foundayo launch — when oral GLP-1 competition from Viking/Roche eventually pressures pricing, IRA drug-price negotiation looms ~2028–2030, and the tirzepatide patent cliff (~2036) compresses the multiple to pharma-peer levels?

**Bull**: Tirzepatide has 10+ year patent runway; Foundayo (approved, launched) extends the franchise into oral without cannibalizing injectable; manufacturing moat ($50B+) creates 3–5 year capacity advantage no competitor replicates; retatrutide (26.6% weight loss) is the pipeline behind the pipeline.

**Bear**: Multiple oral GLP-1 competitors in late-stage trials; IRA is a rising pricing overhang; manufacturing buildout absorbs FCF; a pullback from the $1,249 peak despite record fundamentals signals market concern about competitive intensity.

| **Eli Lilly** | Mounjaro (T2D), Zepbound (obesity), Foundayo (oral) | All launched | — |

**Three structural differentiators vs. all competitors:**
1. **Efficacy lead + oral first-mover**: Best injectable (tirzepatide) AND first oral (Foundayo) — a fleet position no competitor has

3. **NVO competitive deterioration as corroboration**: Novo guiding –5.6% FY2026 revenue against LLY's +23–27% is the sharpest available evidence that tirzepatide is capturing durable market share

**Moat verdict:** Wide, but concentrated. Manufacturing investment is the most defensible structural differentiator. Concentrated franchise risk is real (~61% tirzepatide) but manageable given decade+ patent runway and pipeline depth.

## 9. Industry-Specific Macro Analysis

**1. GLP-1 / Obesity Market Penetration — dominant secular driver**
Global obesity candidates for pharmacotherapy: 500–750M adults; current GLP-1 penetration <5%. U.S. employer insurance coverage for GLP-1 obesity drugs: ~40% of large employers (2025) vs. 15% (2022). CMS/Medicare expansion could add 5–10M patients if legislated. Penetration curve 5% → 20% over 10 years is the structural thesis.

**2. U.S. Drug Pricing (IRA)**
Inflation Reduction Act: CMS negotiates Medicare pricing for small molecules 7 years post-launch. Tirzepatide trigger: ~2028–2030. Foundayo trigger: ~2033–2035. Retatrutide (biologic): 13-year window — strategically more attractive for pricing durability. Management has been vocal opposing IRA at industry level. Headwind is real but manageable if volume growth outpaces price compression.

**3. Manufacturing as Strategic Infrastructure**
For GLP-1s, the moat increasingly includes *supply*. The 2023–2024 period validated that manufacturing capacity was genuinely binding. The $50B+ investment reframes LLY from a cost-center manufacturer to a drug company where capacity is a competitive weapon. Any new oral GLP-1 entrant faces the same 3–5 year supply-scaling challenge LLY has already solved.

**4. Global Reimbursement Trajectory**
Europe, Japan, EM are 2–5 years behind the U.S. on GLP-1 reimbursement. Each country approval adds a multi-year revenue ramp largely uncorrelated with U.S. competitive dynamics. This is a decade-long secular tailwind independent of Novo/Viking competitive intensity.
