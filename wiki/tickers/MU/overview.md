## Business in One Line

Micron is the only U.S. manufacturer of DRAM and NAND flash memory, increasingly dominant in High Bandwidth Memory (HBM3e/HBM4) for AI accelerators — the company is transitioning from commodity cyclical to AI infrastructure critical path.

---

## Moat

**Narrow — HBM oligopoly (3 players) but underlying DRAM is commodity.**

### What MU has
- **HBM barrier to entry**: HBM3e and HBM4 manufacturing requires TSMC CoWoS packaging + deep integration with GPU/AI chip vendors; only SK Hynix, Samsung, and Micron certified for Nvidia H100/H200/GB200 HBM supply
- **U.S. manufacturing footprint**: Only U.S.-based DRAM/NAND maker; national security tailwind (CHIPS Act $6.1B grant) → export control-resilient supply to U.S. hyperscalers
- **Customer diversification**: HBM supply to Nvidia, AMD, Intel Gaudi, and custom ASIC makers — not dependent on single hyperscaler
- **20nm DRAM process leadership**: Closing gap with SK Hynix in leading-edge nodes

### What MU doesn't have
- **HBM market leadership**: SK Hynix holds ~40% HBM market share, Samsung ~40%, Micron ~20% — #3 position means price-taker in tight supply
- **Commodity DRAM moat**: Standard DRAM is priced on spot market; margins collapse in oversupply
- **NAND differentiation**: QLC NAND is largely undifferentiated; pure commodity
- **Vertical integration**: Fabless customers can switch NAND/DRAM suppliers when capacity frees up

### Verdict
**Narrow moat** — valid for HBM supply (2026–2028 design cycle lock-in), no moat for commodity DRAM/NAND. The structural bull case requires HBM3e/HBM4 to remain capacity-constrained through FY2027.
