## 1. Why Does This Company Exist? + Pivotal Investment Question

Micron Technology manufactures DRAM (Dynamic RAM), NAND flash, and High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) — the three fundamental memory/storage tiers inside every computing system. As of 2026, Micron is the only U.S.-based manufacturer of DRAM, making it a critical national security asset during the AI infrastructure buildout. The strategic imperative is clear: training and serving large AI models requires memory bandwidth that only HBM3e and HBM4 can deliver, and only three companies in the world (SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron) have the process technology to manufacture it.

The current thesis is a structural transition: Micron is shifting from a commodity cyclical (traditional DRAM/NAND priced on spot markets, margins collapsing in oversupply) to an AI infrastructure critical-path supplier with contracted HBM revenues and premium pricing power. Q3 FY2026 (ended May 31, 2026) showed $41.46B revenue and 84.6% gross margin in a single quarter — numbers that would be elite even for software companies.

**Pivotal Investment Question:** Will Micron's HBM3e/HBM4 revenue sustain ≥30% HBM market share through FY2028, maintaining extraordinary margins (>60% GM) long enough to justify the current $931B valuation before the next traditional DRAM/NAND oversupply cycle begins?

**Current milestone status:**
- ✅ HBM3e certified for Nvidia H100/H200 and GB200 Blackwell
- ✅ Q3 FY2026: Revenue $41.46B, Gross Margin 84.6%, Net Income $28.24B
- ✅ Net cash position: ~$19.6B (from -$5.6B net debt a year ago)

## 4. Revenue Mix & Business Model

### Revenue Segments

| Segment | Description | FY2025 Revenue | FY2026E Trajectory |
|---------|-------------|---------------|-------------------|
| DRAM | Server, PC, mobile DRAM | ~$26B | Significant; HBM is sub-segment |
| HBM3e / HBM4 | AI accelerator memory | Rapid ramp in FY25 H2 | Dominant driver of margin expansion |
| NAND | Enterprise SSD, consumer flash | ~$8B | Stable; margins lower than DRAM |
| Embedded | Automotive, industrial IoT | ~$3B | Stable |

### Business Model

Micron is an **integrated device manufacturer (IDM)** — it designs and fabricates its own chips, unlike fabless companies. This creates:
- Massive CapEx requirements ($12–20B/year in fabs and equipment)
- Fixed cost leverage in upcycles (extreme margin expansion when ASPs rise)
- Devastating losses in downcycles (2023: -$5.83B net loss)

HBM3e/HBM4 is sold on **long-term supply agreements** with Nvidia, AMD, and others — more predictable than spot DRAM. This contractual revenue is the key structural change from prior cycles.

## 5. Competitive Moat

### What MU has
- **HBM technology barrier**: CoWoS and 3D stacking for HBM4 requires years of process development; only SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron have it
- **CHIPS Act strategic positioning**: $6.1B DOE grant + U.S. government preference for domestic memory supply → long-term structural tailwind for Micron vs. Korean peers in defense/government contracts
- **HBM4 differentiation attempt**: Micron is targeting HBM4 with custom architecture optimized for Nvidia Blackwell B300/B400 variants — if design wins confirmed, moat strengthens

### What MU doesn't have
- **HBM market share leadership**: SK Hynix leads at ~40%; Samsung recovering aggressively; Micron #3 with ~20%
- **Commodity moat**: Standard DDR5/LPDDR5 is pure commodity; any oversupply erases pricing power
- **Software/recurring revenue**: Pure hardware — no subscription layer

### Direct Competitor Analysis

| Competitor | DRAM Share | HBM Share | Strategic Position |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|-------------------|
| SK Hynix | ~35% | ~40% | #1 in HBM, most advanced HBM4 |
| Samsung | ~40% | ~40% | Ramping HBM3e aggressively in 2026 |
| **Micron** | **~25%** | **~20%** | #3 HBM, U.S.-only DRAM manufacturer |
| Western Digital | 0% DRAM | 0% HBM | NAND-only competitor |

**Verdict**: **Narrow moat** — defensible for HBM supply through FY2028 design cycles, no moat for commodity DRAM. Samsung's HBM recovery is the primary competitive risk.

## 9. Industry-Specific Macro Analysis

**Memory TAM**: Global DRAM TAM ~$130B (FY2026E), growing at ~25% CAGR driven by AI server memory; HBM sub-TAM ~$20–30B in FY2026, targeted to reach $100B+ by FY2028 per Micron guidance.

### End-market dynamics

| Market | FY2026 Trend | Key Driver |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| AI Server (HBM) | 🟢 Explosive | Nvidia Blackwell deployments; hyperscaler CapEx at record |
| Data Center DRAM (DDR5) | 🟡 Strong | AI rack memory expansion beyond HBM |
| PC/Consumer DRAM | 🟡 Stable | PC refresh cycle modest; no AI-driven surge |
| Mobile DRAM | 🟡 Stable | LPDDR5x for on-device AI phones modest lift |
| NAND (Enterprise SSD) | 🟠 Mixed | AI training storage grows; consumer NAND competitive |

**Key competitive/pricing dynamic**: HBM3e is priced at 5–10× the ASP of equivalent DRAM capacity, explaining the extraordinary margin expansion. The risk is that Samsung's aggressive HBM3e yield improvements in 2026 H2 begin eroding that pricing premium. SK Hynix and Micron have historically kept HBM pricing disciplined; Samsung's return to HBM competitiveness breaks the oligopoly dynamic.

**Policy**: CHIPS Act + IDA (Ireland) + DOE grants = ~$9B total federal support for Micron's U.S. fab expansion. This provides cost subsidies that partially offset CapEx drag.
