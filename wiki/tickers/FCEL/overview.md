## Business in One Line

## Moat

**Very narrow, with one structural edge.**

### What FCEL has
- Proprietary MCFC stack technology (50+ years of carbonate chemistry IP; difficult to replicate)
- Long-term power purchase agreements (PPAs) locking in recurring service revenue
- SureSource platform scalability (250kW modules → multi-MW stationary deployments)
- DOE Advanced Technology contract revenue stream (non-commercial, government-funded R&D)
- Carbon-capture-compatible electrochemistry (potential future differentiation if carbon markets mature)

### What FCEL doesn't have
- Positive gross margins at any point in the last 4 fiscal years
- Scale advantage: BE ($86.3B market cap, 30%+ gross margin) dwarfs FCEL by ~75× in market cap with superior unit economics
- Technology breadth: SOFC (Bloom Energy) and PEMFC (Plug Power) serve more use cases than MCFC
- Customer diversity: significant concentration in utility and municipal contracts
- Capital efficiency: 3-year cumulative split-adjusted dilution of +292% indicates chronic equity dependence

### Verdict
**Narrow moat in MCFC IP; no moat in unit economics.** The electrochemical IP is real but has not translated to positive gross margin at any scale over 50+ years of commercial operation. Positive gross margin is the single threshold between "speculative lottery ticket" and "investable fuel cell company."
