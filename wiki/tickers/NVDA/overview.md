## Moat

**Wide — the most defensible software-hardware moat in enterprise technology.**

### What NVDA has
- **CUDA ecosystem**: 20-year head start; millions of developer-hours embedded in libraries, frameworks, and production pipelines. Switching cost = rewriting all AI training code.
- **~80% AI GPU market share**: Dominant position in H100/H200/B200 Blackwell generation
- **Hardware-software co-optimization**: cuDNN, TensorRT, NEMO, Isaac, Omniverse all deepen lock-in
- **NVLink/NVSwitch**: Proprietary multi-GPU interconnect enabling 100K+ GPU clusters — only NVDA can build this today
- **HBM integration leadership**: SK Hynix/Micron HBM allocation priority; closest supply chain relationship
- **Net cash ~$51.2B**: Firepower for buybacks, acquisition, and R&D investment
- **Blackwell ramp**: B200 architecture (2025–2026) with 30x inferencing performance vs H100; Rubin (2026–2027) already in development

### What NVDA doesn't have
- **Fab ownership**: 100% TSMC-dependent (N3/N4 processes); single point of supply failure
- **China revenue**: H100/H800 banned 2023; H20 restricted 2024; China was ~20% of data center revenue pre-restriction
- **Guaranteed moat duration**: Custom ASIC threat from hyperscalers (Google TPU, Amazon Trainium, Microsoft Athena) growing from ~15% to projected 25%+ of AI training silicon by FY2028

### Verdict
**Wide moat, but not impregnable.** CUDA lock-in is real and durable (5–10 year horizon). The primary risk is not AMD — it's hyperscaler custom silicon gradually substituting GPU training workloads. NVDA's response (Blackwell/Rubin performance leap + inference economics) is credible for the 2–3 year window.

## Pivotal Investment Question

> **Can NVDA sustain 70%+ gross margins through the Rubin/next-gen architecture cycle as hyperscaler custom ASICs scale from ~15% to ~25%+ of AI training silicon by FY2028 — or will margin compression and share loss force a multiple de-rating below 20x forward earnings?**

The bull case doesn't require NVDA to win 100% of AI silicon forever — it requires only that CUDA lock-in and inferencing economics keep gross margins above 65% while the TAM expands from $300B to $700B+. The bear case requires ASICs to reach a capability/cost crossover for general-purpose training workloads before NVDA's next architecture leap.
