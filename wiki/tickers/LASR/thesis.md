## 1. Why Does This Company Exist? + Pivotal Investment Question

**Raison d'être**: nLIGHT designs, manufactures, and sells semiconductor and fiber lasers across three markets: Aerospace & Defense (directed-energy weapons, laser sensing/LIDAR for defense platforms), Industrial (cutting, welding, additive manufacturing), and Microfabrication (semiconductor and electronics processing). Unlike COHR/LITE, which sell into AI-datacenter optical interconnects, nLIGHT's growth engine is **defense directed-energy** — high-power lasers used in counter-drone and missile-defense systems — a theme distinct from, but adjacent to, the broader photonics/optics narrative this wiki already covers (COHR, AXTI, NVTS, POET are AI-datacenter or power-semiconductor plays; nLIGHT is defense-laser-first).

nLIGHT is one of a small number of US-based, vertically-integrated laser manufacturers with both semiconductor laser diode fabrication (Vancouver, WA) and fiber laser systems capability — a combination that matters for defense customers who require domestic, non-China supply chains for directed-energy weapon components.

**Strategic position as of 2026-08-18**:
- Q2 FY2026 (June quarter) revenue $82.6M, +33.8% YoY, beat consensus ($68.2M avg estimate implied ~$74M actual expectation — reported well above)
- Record Aerospace & Defense segment revenue, driven by new directed-energy contract awards
- **Joint Laser Weapon System (JLWS) contract awarded — ceiling above $600M**, expected to begin contributing revenue from Q3 2026 onward
- Q3 FY2026 guidance cut to **$63–73M** (below Q2's $82.6M) — management attributed this explicitly to **China-linked supply chain delays**, not to softening defense or industrial demand
- Non-GAAP EPS $0.15 (in line with consensus), GAAP net loss narrowed to -$1.34M (vs -$6.87M a year ago)
- Raised ~$150M+ in equity in Q1 2026 (shares outstanding jumped from 51.2M to 57.7M, +12.7%, cash rose from $99M to $296M) — balance sheet is now net-cash and well-funded for the JLWS ramp
- Stock crashed -18.4% to -25% in the days following the 8/6 print; down -41% from the June ATH of $84.95

**Pivotal Question**: *Will the China-linked supply chain constraint flagged for Q3 2026 resolve within 1–2 quarters (allowing the JLWS ramp and record A&D bookings to convert into visible revenue growth by Q1–Q2 FY2027), or is nLIGHT's laser-diode/optics supply chain structurally exposed to China in a way that caps quarterly revenue and margin expansion through 2027 regardless of order backlog?*

## 5. Competitive Moat

**What makes nLIGHT's position defensible**:
- One of a small number of US-based vertically-integrated laser diode + fiber laser manufacturers — matters structurally for defense customers requiring ITAR-compliant, non-China-sourced components
- Direct relationship and now a large-ceiling ($600M+) program (JLWS) with a defense prime — multi-year program revenue, high switching costs once qualified into a weapons platform
- 25+ years of laser-diode fabrication IP (founded 2000)

**What nLIGHT doesn't have**:
- Scale versus IPG Photonics (larger, more diversified fiber laser franchise) or Coherent (larger, broader compound-semiconductor base)
- Immunity from its own supply chain — the Q2 guidance cut shows nLIGHT is exposed to China-linked inputs even as it sells a "domestic supply chain" story to defense customers
- Consistent profitability — still GAAP loss-making every year in the dataset

**Direct Competitors**

| Company | Business Line Overlap | Comment |
|---------|----------------------|---------|
| IPG Photonics (IPGP) | Fiber lasers, industrial | Larger scale ($1.07B TTM rev), diversified, profitable |
| Coherent (COHR) | Compound semiconductor lasers, photonics | Much larger ($7.1B rev), AI-datacenter focus, not defense-directed-energy-first |
| Lumentum (LITE) | Photonics, some defense/aerospace via commercial lasers segment | AI-optics primary growth driver, not directed energy |
| II-VI legacy defense optics units (now part of COHR) | Overlapping defense optics | Indirect competitor via COHR's aerospace & defense segment |
| Northrop Grumman / Lockheed Martin internal directed-energy programs | In-house laser weapon development | Primes sometimes build in-house rather than buy — key competitive risk |

**What is eroding the moat**: Defense primes building directed-energy capability in-house reduces addressable market for external suppliers like nLIGHT; and — ironically — the very supply chain the Q2 guidance flagged shows the "domestic defense supplier" narrative has a China-dependency crack in it today.

**Verdict**: **Narrow moat.** The JLWS contract and defense qualification represent a real, multi-year revenue lock-in once fully ramped, but nLIGHT is a mid-scale player relative to IPGP/COHR and carries a supply-chain vulnerability that directly contradicts its "domestic defense supply chain" positioning. Time horizon to verify: JLWS ramp visibility through Q1–Q2 FY2027 will show whether this moat converts into durable, high-margin defense revenue or remains contract-lumpy.

## 9. Industry-Specific Macro Analysis

**Market sizing**: Directed-energy weapons (laser-based counter-drone, missile defense) is a fast-growing niche within the broader ~$400B+ global defense electronics market; specific TAM estimates for directed-energy vary widely ($1–5B by 2030 depending on program funding pace) given the program-based (not commercial) nature of demand. Industrial laser market (nLIGHT's other segment) is a more mature, cyclical ~$15–20B global market growing low-single-digits.

**End-market dynamics**:
- **Aerospace & Defense (primary growth driver)**: Counter-drone and directed-energy programs have accelerated funding given the visibility of drone warfare in recent conflicts (Ukraine, Middle East). JLWS is nLIGHT's flagship program; additional laser-sensing/LIDAR defense contracts referenced but not itemized.
- **Industrial (cutting/welding)**: Cyclical, correlated with global manufacturing capex; has been the weak segment historically (FY2023–2024 revenue declines were largely industrial-driven).
- **Microfabrication (semiconductor processing lasers)**: Smaller segment, tied to semiconductor capex cycle — could be a tailwind if AI-driven fab capex spills into laser-based processing tools, but this is not the current thesis driver.

**Competitive/pricing dynamic**: Unlike the AI-optics photonics names in this wiki (COHR, AXTI) where the dynamic is Chinese optical-module price competition at hyperscalers, nLIGHT's competitive dynamic is **defense-program-specific** — competing for prime-contractor qualification rather than commodity pricing. However, the China exposure on the *input* side (components/materials) is a structural parallel to the broader "China dependency" risk seen across the sector's supply chains, even for defense-focused names.

**Regulatory/policy tailwinds**: US defense budget growth and explicit DoD emphasis on directed-energy and counter-drone systems (public FY2026/2027 budget requests) are a structural tailwind. Regulatory risk: any US restriction on China-sourced defense-adjacent components would be a forcing function that could *help* (justify higher-cost domestic sourcing, price umbrella) or *hurt* (near-term supply disruption, exactly what happened in Q2 guidance) nLIGHT depending on timing.
