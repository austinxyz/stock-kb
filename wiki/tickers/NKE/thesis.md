## 1. Why Does This Company Exist? + Pivotal Investment Question

Nike exists to bring inspiration and innovation to every athlete in the world. Founded in 1964 as Blue Ribbon Sports (renamed Nike in 1971), it became the world's #1 sports brand by combining elite athlete endorsements (Michael Jordan, Ronaldo, LeBron), technological innovation (Air cushioning, React foam, ZoomX carbon plates), and global distribution across 190+ countries. For 30+ years it was the standard-bearer for premium athletic performance; Jordan Brand (born 1984) evolved into an autonomous sneaker luxury franchise generating ~$6B in annual revenue within the Nike ecosystem.

**The current inflection**: Three years of strategic misstep under CEO John Donahoe (2020–2024) — over-rotation to direct-to-consumer digital, cutting key wholesale partners (Foot Locker, DSW, etc.), under-investing in performance running, and failing to maintain product freshness — resulted in revenue declining from $51.4B (FY2024) to $46.3B (FY2025, -9.8% YoY). Elliott Hill, a 32-year Nike veteran, returned as CEO in October 2024 with a mandate to rebuild wholesale relationships, re-energize sport performance, and restore brand cultural relevance.

**Pivotal Investment Question**: **Will Elliott Hill's turnaround restore Nike's revenue growth above $50B and operating margins above 10% by FY2027, or will tariff headwinds (17%+ effective US tariff rate post-2025 policy), China slowdown (~15% of revenue), and structural competitive erosion from HOKA/On Running/Adidas keep Nike in margin compression through FY2028?**

Current milestone status:
- ✅ CEO transition completed (Oct 2024): Hill restored, Donahoe exited
- ✅ Revenue stabilization: Q3 FY26 (Feb 2026) revenue essentially flat YoY (+0.1%) vs -10% a year ago
- ✅ 4 consecutive EPS beats (Q4 FY25–Q3 FY26, all vs lowered bar)
- ⏳ Revenue return to growth (FY2027+): Not yet confirmed
- ⏳ Operating margin recovery above 10%: FY2025 was 8.0%, FY2026E consensus ~9%
- ⏳ Wholesale channel rebuild: Partner restocking underway but not quantified
- ⏳ China recovery: Greater China revenue still under pressure from local competition (Li Ning, Anta)

*Converse is a separate brand but included in Nike, Inc. consolidated reporting.

### Business Model
Nike is a **design-and-market** company (not a manufacturer). Core model:
- **Wholesale (~55% of revenue)**: DSG, Foot Locker, JD Sports, Dick's, Nordstrom — higher volume, lower margin
- **Direct-to-Consumer (~45%)**: Nike.com, Nike apps, Nike-owned retail stores — lower volume, higher margin
- Hill's strategy: rebalance toward wholesale (Donahoe had cut wholesale; Hill is rebuilding these relationships)
- Gross margin under pressure because DTC mix was supposed to improve margins but the transition cost too much in wholesale revenue

## 5. Competitive Moat

### Defensible Advantages
- **Brand equity**: ~$50B brand value (Interbrand); Jordan Brand is a standalone cultural phenomenon
- **Athlete IP lock-in**: 30+ year relationships with top athletes across all major sports create irreplaceable cultural associations
- **Endorsement network depth**: NFL, NBA, NFL, USATF contracts; college team licensing
- **Manufacturing partnerships**: Trusted capacity at Pou Chen (Vietnam), Pan Pacific (Indonesia) — preferred production allocation
- **Digital data**: 80M+ Nike App users; SNKRS shoe release data; Nike Run Club GPS data — consumer behavior insights

### Competitive Threats
| Competitor | Threat Area | Severity |
|-----------|-------------|----------|
| Adidas (ADDYY) | Streetwear/lifestyle relevance (Samba era), YEEZY revival | High |
| On Running (ONON) | Premium running performance (Cloudboom Echo) | High |
| HOKA (DECK subsidiary) | Trail/road running performance; unit economics | High |
| New Balance | Retro fashion + Boston-based running credibility | Medium |
| Lululemon (LULU) | Women's premium athletic lifestyle | Medium |
| Li Ning / Anta | China market share — local patriotism over Nike | High in China |

### What Is Eroding the Moat
- 3-year DTC push alienated wholesale partners, giving competitors shelf space
- Under-investment in running performance ceded Nike's core identity to HOKA/On
- Jordan Brand's licensing saturation is diluting perceived exclusivity
- Gen Z cultural disconnect (Adidas Samba, New Balance 990 dominate streetwear)

### Verdict
**Wide moat** — brand IP and athlete relationships are extremely durable over decades. However, the moat is currently being harvested rather than invested in. Hill's investment in wholesale and product innovation is the right prescription but requires 2–3 years to show results. Re-verify moat strength at FY2027 earnings (May 2027).

**Most Underappreciated Risk**: The Vietnam tariff risk is structural, not cyclical. With 40%+ of Nike footwear manufactured in Vietnam, even a moderate tariff increase (+10–15 percentage points) would compress gross margin by 200–400bps permanently, wiping out most of the expected margin recovery. Nike cannot quickly relocate manufacturing (18–24 month supply chain migration minimum). If the Trump-era tariff policy is not resolved by FY2027, Nike's bear case becomes the base case.

## 9. Industry-Specific Macro Analysis

**TAM**: Global athletic footwear market ~$120B (2025), growing ~5% annually to ~$160B by 2030 (Statista/Nike IR). Apparel adds another $200B. Nike's current penetration is ~12% of combined market — stable to slightly declining.

**End-Market Dynamics**:
- **Running performance**: Fastest growing sub-category; dominated by HOKA (Deckers) and On Running; Nike catching up with next-gen ZoomX
- **Basketball**: Jordan Brand stable; Nike still dominant; Adidas competing with Harden/T-Mac lines
- **Soccer/Football**: Nike vs Adidas duopoly; roughly 50/50 global share; durable
- **Lifestyle/Fashion**: Most competitive; Adidas winning with Samba/Campus retro; New Balance 990v5 viral; Nike losing share

**Tariff Environment (Critical)**:
- ~40% of footwear made in Vietnam (VN tariff was 12.5% pre-2025, now in flux with Trump 2.0 policies)
- Nike pays ~15–20% of COGS in tariffs currently; any escalation directly hits gross margin
- Competitors (Adidas, LULU) also exposed to tariffs but less concentrated in Vietnam

**China Competition**:
- Li Ning and Anta have benefited from post-2021 "buy Chinese" sentiment
- Nike Greater China revenue peaked ~$10.4B (FY2019), declined to ~$7.1B (FY2025)
- Recovery requires cultural rehabilitation, not just distribution

Any Q4 FY26 positive surprise will be met with disproportionate price reaction due to short-covering (short interest ~5.3% float). **Verdict**: Behavioral edge exists for contrarian buyers near 52wk lows; narrative is too pessimistic on brand durability.

### A — Analytical
Revenue declined -9.8% in FY2025 but **revenue is now flat YoY** (Q3 FY26 +0.1%). Earnings beats have been driven by cost cuts (SG&A -3%) not revenue recovery. Analysts have cut FY2027E from $2.38 to $1.85 over 90 days. Consensus now assumes continuing tariff headwinds (-$0.3/share impact) and China stagnation. Bulls miss: wholesale channel refill could add $2-3B revenue in FY2027 when partners' Nike allocation normalizes. Bears miss: dividend yield 3.65% and $9B cash provide price floor.
**Verdict**: Consensus too bearish on revenue recovery timeline; too optimistic on tariff resolution (50/50 at best).

Key information advantages: (1) Nike doesn't publish quarterly wholesale orders data — trade data from port tracking shows improving Vietnam exports, suggesting restocking cycle underway; (2) SNKRS app sell-through data (not public) can be estimated via secondary market prices (Jordan 1 resale prices recovering from FY2024 lows); (3) China market share data (quarterly Euromonitor) is the leading indicator for Greater China revenue recovery, 1-2 quarters ahead.
**Verdict**: No public information edge; tariff policy (binary) is the unobservable variable that dominates.
